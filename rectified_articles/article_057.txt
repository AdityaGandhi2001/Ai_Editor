UK home secretary threatens India with visa restrictions over migrants

Synopsis
The United Kingdom's new Home Secretary, Shabana Mahmood, has warned that India and other nations could face new visa restrictions if they don't cooperate in taking back illegal migrants. Mahmood, who was appointed in 2025, is prepared to risk diplomatic tensions over her deportation plans. The warning comes after the Five Eyes intelligence alliance, which now comprises the UK, the US, Australia, Canada and New Zealand, agreed on new principles to pressure countries into accepting their citizens back.

India, Pakistan and Nigeria most resistant to taking back migrants
According to The Times, India, Pakistan and Nigeria are among the most resistant to taking back overstaying migrants. In fact, India tops the list with 20,706 Indians overstaying their visas in the year to 2020, the report claims. Meanwhile, Pakistan has refused to take back foreign criminals including Qari Abdul Rauf and Adil Khan, who renounced their Pakistani citizenship.

Mahmood's warning could strain ties with India: Migration experts
Migration experts have cautioned that Mahmood's warning could strain ties with key partners like India; Dr Peter Walsh of Oxford University's Migration Observatory said India has a "dim view" of bargaining tactics that involve these kinds of threats. He added that India has lobbied the UK government for privileged access to the UK's visa system.

Mahmood promises to go 'further and faster' than predecessor
In her first public comments as home secretary, Mahmood warned senior Home Office staff to "bring their A-game—or leave" and said she would go "further and faster" than her predecessor, Yvette Cooper, on tackling migrant crossings this year, which hit a record 30,165.
